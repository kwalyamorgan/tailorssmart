The TailorSmart Women Empowerment Initiative was implemented in Mukuru Kwa Njenga slums, Nairobi County, to address socioeconomic exclusion among vulnerable women. The project focused on equipping five women with tailoring and entrepreneurship skills, supported by provision of sewing machines, fabrics, and mentorship.
The main objectives were to empower women with marketable skills, enhance household financial security, and promote sustainable livelihoods. Target beneficiaries were low-income women, particularly single mothers, who face challenges of unemployment, unstable incomes, and limited access to vocational training.
Key activities included beneficiary selection, procurement of sewing machines and materials, delivery of a structured two-week tailoring and business training program, and distribution of start-up kits. Adjustments were made to accommodate flexible training hours and ensure active participation.
The project outcomes were significant: four women acquired tailoring and business skills, each received a sewing machine, and immediate income-generating opportunities were established. Beyond individual impact, the initiative fostered community recognition, strengthened peer support networks, and contributed to Sustainable Development Goals on poverty reduction, gender equality, and decent work.
Project Background
Location of the Project

Geographic and Demographic Description:  
Mukuru Kwa Njenga is a densely populated informal settlement located in Nairobi County. The community is characterized by high poverty levels, inadequate housing, and limited access to formal employment opportunities. Economic activities are largely informal, including small-scale trading, casual labor, and artisanal services. Vulnerabilities include unemployment, financial dependency, poor sanitation, and limited access to healthcare and education. Social dynamics are marked by strong community networks, resilience, and reliance on grassroots initiatives for survival and empowerment.
Project Objectives
General Objective  
To empower four women from Mukuru Kwa Njenga slums with tailoring and entrepreneurship skills, enabling them to achieve sustainable income generation and improved household resilience.
Specific Objectives
•	Train four women in garment making, sewing machine operation, and tailoring techniques.
•	Provide each beneficiary with a sewing machine and starter materials to support immediate business start-up.
•	Deliver business skills training, including financial literacy, branding, and market access strategies.
•	Foster confidence, self-sufficiency, and peer support networks among the women to strengthen community resilience.
Target Beneficiaries
Number: 4 women
Profile: Low-income female residents of Mukuru Kwa Njenga, prioritizing single mothers and households with limited income.
Expected Change
•	Increased household financial security through sustainable income generation.
•	Improved access to education, nutrition, and healthcare for families supported by the beneficiaries.
•	Strengthened community networks and local availability of tailoring services.

Challenges and Lessons Learned
Challenges Faced
•	Trainer fees exceeded budget due to extended mentorship hours beyond the initial plan.
•	Branding costs were higher than anticipated to achieve professional visibility for the shopfront.
•	Domestic responsibilities of beneficiaries occasionally conflicted with training schedules, risking drop outs.
•	Market access limitations for newly trained women, with competition from established tailors in the area.
•	Resource strain from higher fabric consumption during practice sessions.
Mitigation Strategies
•	Negotiated flexible payment terms with the trainer to accommodate extended hours.
•	Adjusted branding scope to prioritize essential visibility while maintaining professional standards.
•	Introduced flexible training hours (morning instruction, observation, afternoon practice) to balance domestic duties.
•	Linked beneficiaries with local schools and community networks for uniform contracts and garment repair opportunities.
•	Monitored fabric use closely and encouraged practice on smaller items to reduce wastage.
 Lessons Learned
•	Planning: Budget allocations should anticipate possible overruns in mentorship and branding.
•	Community Engagement: Early involvement of local leaders and the pastor strengthened recruitment and credibility.
•	Time Management: Structured daily routines (instruction, observation, practice) ensured consistent skill acquisition.
•	Resource Utilization: Careful monitoring of consumables like fabrics is essential to avoid overspending.
•	Sustainability: Linking beneficiaries to community markets and networks is critical for long term success.

Recommendations
Improving Future CAP Projects
•	Enhanced Budget Planning: Anticipate possible overruns in trainer fees, branding, and consumables by including a contingency allocation.
•	Extended Training Duration: Consider stretching training beyond four weeks to allow deeper mastery and more practice time.
•	Structured Monitoring: Introduce mid training evaluations and post training follow ups to track progress and ensure accountability.
Recommendations for the Community & Stakeholders
•	Community Partnerships: Strengthen collaboration with local schools, churches, and small businesses to secure uniform contracts and garment repair opportunities.
•	Mentorship Networks: Engage experienced tailors and entrepreneurs as ongoing mentors for beneficiaries.
•	Faith Based Support: Leverage the local pastor’s involvement to enhance credibility, mobilize community trust, and sustain engagement.
Opportunities for Scaling & Sustainability
•	TailorSmart Network Expansion: Scale the initiative by recruiting more women in Mukuru Kwa Njenga and neighboring slums, creating a cooperative model.
•	Microfinance Linkages: Partner with Women Enterprise Fund and other microfinance institutions to provide small loans for business growth.
•	Shopfront Visibility: Use branding and signage to establish TailorSmart as a recognized hub for women’s empowerment and tailoring services.
•	Peer to Peer Training: Empower the initial four beneficiaries to train new cohorts, multiplying impact at low cost.
